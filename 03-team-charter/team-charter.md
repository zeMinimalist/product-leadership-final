# Team Charter: Meridian Foundations

> Module 3 · Lead and Develop High-Performing Teams, ★ Deliverable 3

These are proposed operating agreements for the Meridian scenario, not claims about established company policy.

## 1. What We Own

| Area | We own it | We influence it (don't own) |
|---|---|---|
| Field workflow | PM 1: adoption and mobile issue capture, assignment, status, and confirmed resolution; offline capture and minimal foreman participation. | Customer site operations and subcontractor practices. |
| Enterprise compatibility | PM 2: integration requirements, data mappings, and acceptance criteria for entering field data once and preserving permissions and records. Engineering owns implementation. | Enterprise/platform roadmaps outside the initiative; sales and customer success relationships. |
| Combined outcome and capacity | Product leader: field adoption, less chasing, and no duplicate reporting; allocation of approved initiative capacity. | Sponsor-level budget, strategy, and commercial authority. |

> Our mission in one line: Help field superintendents coordinate site issues once, get accountable action, and confirm resolution without chasing group texts or reentering updates.

### Scope and strategic focus

Foundations owns routine, photo-based site-issue coordination for field superintendents on existing US commercial projects worth $50M or more: capture → assignment → status → confirmed resolution. PM 1 owns field adoption and the mobile experience, including offline capture and minimal foreman participation. PM 2 owns enterprise integration requirements, data mappings, and compatibility acceptance criteria so field data is entered once and required permissions and records are preserved; engineering owns implementation. The product leader is accountable for the combined outcome. Current focus: reliable offline capture and synchronization, and foreman participation through resolution. Photo markup is supporting work, not an independent strategic commitment. These are proposed charter agreements for the scenario, not established Meridian policy.

### Out of scope and routing

New executive dashboards, time tracking, and dashboard-driven data-model migration are outside Foundations this quarter. PM 2 owns intake and routing of those requests for enterprise/platform consideration; routing does not commit another team’s capacity or delivery. PM 2 also routes formal RFI requirements through the customer’s existing RFI process. A broad standalone field platform is excluded. Daily-log simplification and schedule notifications remain conditional Next work. Existing enterprise functionality remains supported. The engineering lead and customer success triage service incidents, including the broken Procore integration, through the existing support process; the product leader approves any non-emergency displacement of Now work. Physical site emergencies go immediately to the customer’s site emergency/safety procedures and emergency services where appropriate, not the routine issue or business-day escalation workflow. Urgent production, security, or data-loss incidents follow incident response rather than waiting for roadmap approval.

### Cross-boundary calls

Both PMs and the engineering lead must review engineering reallocation, renewal promises involving field scope, shared synchronization or integration changes, permissions, required data, and changes spanning mobile and enterprise workflows before action. Consultation is mandatory, but unanimous PM agreement is not required: the product leader makes the cross-domain priority call within delegated authority. Technical feasibility and mandatory security/compliance controls cannot be waived by a priority decision. Sales cannot commit Foundations scope, dates, or engineering capacity; external commitments require a recorded product-leader decision and engineering feasibility confirmation.

## 2. How We Decide

| Decision type | Who decides | Who's consulted | How we break a tie |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feature scope within agreed domain and capacity | Owning PM | Engineering, design, other PM when affected | Product leader resolves a cross-domain conflict. |
| Technical implementation and release readiness | Engineering lead | Both PMs and relevant technical specialists | Revise scope, sequence, or capacity and reassess; priority does not override technical readiness or mandatory controls. |
| Shared capacity or Now roadmap change within agreed strategy and approved envelope | Product leader | Both PMs and engineering lead; sales/customer success for renewal evidence | Recorded product-leader decision; unanimous PM agreement is not required. |
| Strategy reversal, budget/capacity increase, or commercial commitment beyond leader authority | Executive sponsor or designated delegate | Product leader, both PMs, engineering, relevant commercial stakeholders | Written sponsor decision; no automatic approval or new commitment while pending. |

> Our default: the domain PM decides within agreed scope and capacity; we escalate to the product leader for cross-domain priority conflicts and to the executive sponsor when strategy, the approved resource envelope, or delegated commercial authority would change.

### Authority and segment exceptions

Each PM decides feature scope and sequencing within their agreed domain and allocated capacity after consulting engineering and design. The engineering lead decides implementation and technical release readiness; if a proposal is infeasible, the product leader must change scope, sequencing, or capacity and obtain a fresh assessment, not override readiness. The product leader decides cross-domain priorities, allocation within the approved initiative budget/capacity, and Now roadmap changes that preserve the agreed field-adoption strategy. The executive sponsor approves strategy reversals, increases beyond the approved budget/capacity envelope, and commercial commitments outside the leader’s delegated authority. The $50M threshold is the chosen pilot segment, not a technical restriction: the product leader may approve a documented smaller-project design-partner exception only if it reuses the same workflow without displacing Now work or broadening the initiative; a change to the target-market strategy requires sponsor approval.

### Conflict escalation: who, how, and when

The PM identifying a conflict logs the request, evidence, affected outcome, engineering estimate, displaced work, and time received in a shared decision log. Both PMs and the engineering lead meet within one business day of receipt. For decisions within delegated authority, the product leader makes and records the decision within two business days of the original logged escalation, not two additional days after the meeting. Decisions requiring sponsor authority are escalated in writing by that same deadline; the sponsor or their designated delegate decides within two business days of receiving the written escalation. If the sponsor deadline is missed, the product leader records the delay and provides a daily business-day update; existing priorities remain unchanged and no new commitment is authorized. Silence is never approval. Approved decisions name the owner and revise scope, capacity, and delivery expectations.

### External requests and renewal pressure

Sales and renewal requests enter through PM 2, who logs them on receipt and gathers credible renewal risk, revenue exposure, decision deadline, contractual obligations, and workaround options with sales and customer success. PM 1 assesses field-outcome effects and engineering estimates feasibility, dependencies, and displaced work. The product leader decides or sends a written sponsor escalation within two business days of the request’s logged receipt; incomplete evidence does not reset the clock, and a request may be declined or held without a delivery commitment. Sponsor decisions follow the two-business-day deadline above. Any request displacing Now work needs a documented product-leader trade-off decision; strategy reversals or commitments beyond delegated authority need prior sponsor approval. PM 2 communicates the recorded decision to sales/customer success; only approved scope and feasible delivery expectations may be relayed to the customer. Sales urgency alone cannot redirect engineering or add a promise on unchanged capacity.

### Peer review and resulting refinements

My peer found the scope, PM split, engineering readiness ownership, existing-priorities default, and renewal-evidence bar strong. Reviewing the condensed charter, they questioned joint-call boundaries, sponsor timing, external decision authority, sales commitments, ownership of excluded work, integration ownership, and smaller-project exceptions.

I retained the field-adoption strategy and clarified consultation versus approval, sponsor authority and deadlines, PM 2’s sales intake and routing responsibility, integration requirements, emergency routing, and documented segment exceptions. Existing priorities remain in force while escalations are pending.
