# Financial Model: Meridian Foundations

> Module 5 · Master Product Financials & Strategic Bets

## 1. Business case

### Strategic bet

Fund a bounded offline-first issue-coordination pilot for field superintendents at existing US commercial-construction customers running projects worth $50M or more. Reliable field capture enables repeat issue coordination; increased reliance on Meridian may reduce account cancellations at renewal. The return is attributable retained contracts, not customer time savings treated as revenue. Minimal foreman participation and enterprise synchronization are dependencies. Count the shared workflow’s return once, not separately against each Rock.

> **The case in one paragraph:** Fund a capped learning pilot to test whether reliable offline issue coordination creates repeat field value and strengthens renewals in existing enterprise accounts. At the illustrative scale of 20 accounts, a 5-point attributable retention improvement yields one additional expected renewal, $200,000 retained ARR, and $140,000 annual contribution after margin and additional operating costs. That supports testing, not an unconditional full-build approval: validate the behavior, cost, and renewal mechanism before releasing the remaining $135,000. Customer time savings and app adoption are supporting evidence, not booked Meridian revenue.

### Assumptions, ranked

All new financial inputs below are illustrative planning assumptions, not supplied Meridian data or observed results. Rank them by their effect on the funding conclusion:

1. **Attributable retention improvement:** annual account churn falls from 10% to 5% because the workflow delivers value. This is the load-bearing assumption and must be compared with renewals that would occur anyway.
2. **Eligible renewal cohort:** 20 existing accounts averaging $200,000 ARR each; the model does not identify actual customers.
3. **Contribution margin:** 80% on retained revenue before the initiative’s additional operating costs.
4. **Additional operating costs:** $20,000 annually for synchronization, infrastructure, support, and maintenance, excluding costs already included in the margin.
5. **Investment:** $180,000 total, including a capped $45,000 pilot and $135,000 conditional follow-on. Include cross-functional delivery and shared workflow dependencies, not just coding. Validate scope, estimates, and capacity before spending.
6. **Timing:** benefits emerge at actual renewal dates, not automatically at launch.

### Expected return and timing

At unit level, a 5-percentage-point incremental renewal probability × $200,000 ARR yields $10,000 expected retained ARR per eligible account annually. Across 20 accounts, that is one additional expected renewal and $200,000 retained ARR—not a guarantee of one saved customer.

Annual incremental contribution = ($200,000 × 80%) − $20,000 = **$140,000**. Recovering the $180,000 investment requires **15.4 months of steady-state contribution**. This is not cash payback from project start: build time, adoption, renewal timing, and collections extend actual recovery. No specific realized revenue date can be supported without the cohort’s renewal schedule.

### CFO stress-test

- **Load-bearing assumption:** If the 5-point retention uplift is 20% lower, a 4-point uplift produces $108,000 annual incremental contribution and 20.0 months of steady-state recovery. At 30% lower, a 3.5-point uplift produces $92,000 and 23.5 months—little room above the $90,000 hurdle. These are scenarios, not estimates validated by customer evidence.
- **Retention sensitivity:** If feature-cohort churn is 10 points above the projected 5%, it becomes 15%. Against an unchanged 10% comparison baseline, incremental retained ARR becomes negative $200,000; after margin and additional operating costs, annual incremental contribution is negative $180,000. The case fails. If wider market conditions change both groups, reassess against the changed comparison baseline rather than attribute all movement to the product.
- **Payback and acquisition costs:** This is an existing-account retention investment, not a new-customer acquisition case. No new-customer CAC assumption or LTV:CAC ratio is supported. Separately, a 20% build-cost overrun raises investment to $216,000 and steady-state recovery to 18.5 months at the base contribution. Operating costs and renewal timing still require validation.
- **Kill-criterion assessment:** The operational gate specifies metrics, thresholds, a week-12 deadline, and a financial consequence. The financial gate prevents operational adoption from automatically releasing the remaining budget. The limitations are the unvalidated thresholds and the small pilot’s inability to prove the annual retention effect.

**Funding verdict:** I would approve only the capped $45,000 learning pilot, subject to credible scope and estimates, because the proposed retention economics justify testing but do not yet justify the full $180,000 investment.

**Required evidence before follow-on funding:** Validate attributable renewal improvement and the fully loaded contribution model; do not substitute app usage or customer time savings for Meridian revenue.

## 2. Kill criteria

By the end of pilot week 12, across at least five pilot accounts, require all three:

- At least 60% of eligible issues coordinated end-to-end in Meridian. Include eligible issues handled outside Meridian in the denominator; exclude emergencies and formal RFIs.
- At least 90% of field-captured issues available in the enterprise record without manual reentry.
- At least a 25% reduction in median superintendent time spent chasing ownership and status, against a measured pre-pilot baseline using a consistent measurement method.

**If any threshold is missed, stop further feature investment, withhold the remaining $135,000, and reallocate expansion capacity.** Maintain necessary support and data integrity for existing pilot users. Product owns the gate decision and records results with Finance and engineering before any follow-on budget or headcount commitment.

Passing these operational gates is necessary, not sufficient, for full funding. Release follow-on funding only with a Finance-reviewed case, backed by credible incremental renewal evidence, supporting at least **$90,000 annual incremental contribution**. That is the minimum for recovering the full $180,000 within two years of steady-state benefit, not within two years of project start. If evidence is insufficient, withhold the follow-on; adoption alone is not proof of retention. A five-account pilot cannot establish a reliable 5-point annual churn effect by itself.
