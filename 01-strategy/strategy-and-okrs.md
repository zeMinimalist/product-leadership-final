# Product Strategy One-Pager & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Craft an Advanced Product Strategy, ★ Deliverable 1
>
> Your one spine: the **Playing to Win** cascade, one deliberate **hard no**, and an **OKR cascade** that flows directly from it.
> Draft the cascade + hard no in Sprint 1, then add the OKRs in Sprint 2. The goal: specific enough that a skeptical board member couldn't poke a hole in it.

## 0. Chosen scenario

**Path:** Meridian Foundations (B2B · adoption + expansion)

Field adoption offers a focused strategic challenge: make Meridian useful to the people coordinating site work without sacrificing the enterprise depth existing customers depend on.

**Diagnosis:** Meridian serves office teams, while superintendents and foremen coordinate through phone cameras and group texts. Our provisional bet is to win photo-based site-issue coordination on existing US commercial projects worth $50M or more. The commercial-project focus and workflow are strategic choices to validate, not supplied research findings.

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration**: winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics | Be the tool US commercial-construction superintendents reach for when a site issue threatens progress: capture it once, get it to the responsible person, and know it is resolved without chasing group texts or reentering updates. |
| **Where to play**: segment, geography, channel, use case (the no's matter too) | US general contractors already using Meridian on commercial projects worth $50M or more; field superintendents first, with subcontractor foremen participating. Mobile photo → location and issue → responsible person → resolution confirmation. Reach users through existing customer relationships and job-site onboarding. No initial new-market expansion or broad replacement of field workflows. |
| **How to win**: your differentiator competitors can't easily replicate | Combine phone-camera convenience with existing Meridian project context and the enterprise record, turning a field update into a traceable, assigned issue without a second administrative step. The potential advantage is the field-to-enterprise connection already embedded in customer operations, not photo capture alone. Validate data quality and whether this is easier than texting. |
| **Capabilities required**: what you must be world-class at (build / buy / partner) | Build fast, offline-capable capture, reliable synchronization, and simple assignment and resolution tracking. Reuse project records, permissions, and document-control infrastructure. Buy commodity mobile services where practical. Partner with pilot customers through UX research and customer success to observe work and remove onboarding friction. |
| **Management systems**: the metrics and rituals that reinforce your choices | Weekly pilot reviews of repeat use by superintendent cohort, end-to-end issue adoption, and capture-to-assignment time. Monthly reviews of chasing time, duplicate entry, and unresolved-issue age. Guardrails for synchronization reliability and preservation of permissions and records. Expand only when repeat use and reduced coordination effort improve together; validate baselines before committing targets. |

## 2. Your one hard no

_One valuable thing you are explicitly choosing **not** to do, and why it protects the focus of everything above. This is a deliberate trade-off, not a backlog of deprioritized items._

> We will not build advanced enterprise analytics in this initiative because it would split the 10-person team’s focus without solving the superintendent’s immediate coordination problem. Existing enterprise functionality stays intact. Essential field visibility into unresolved issues by owner and age remains in scope as workflow support, not a separate analytics initiative.

## 3. OKR cascade

_One Objective and three Key Results that flow directly from the cascade. Each KR must be a measurable **outcome**, not an output/milestone._

> **Objective:** Make Meridian Foundations the default way field superintendents on existing US commercial projects worth $50M or more coordinate site issues—without chasing group texts or entering the same update twice.
>
> - **KR1:** Increase the share of eligible site issues coordinated end-to-end in Meridian, from capture through confirmed resolution, from an assumed 10% to a proposed 60% by the end of pilot week 12.
> - **KR2:** Reduce superintendent time spent chasing ownership and status updates from an assumed 60 to a proposed 30 minutes per workday by the end of pilot week 12.
> - **KR3:** Increase the share of field-captured issues available in the enterprise record without manual reentry from an assumed 20% to a proposed 90% by the end of pilot week 12.

**Measurement scope and assumptions:** Pilot job sites over a 12-week evaluation period. The scenario supplies no quantitative workflow baselines: all baselines are illustrative planning assumptions and targets are proposed, not validated Meridian data. Validate baselines and target feasibility before commitment. No kickoff date is set, so deadlines are relative to pilot start.

**Eligible issues:** Routine, photo-documentable coordination issues—not emergencies or formal RFIs requiring separate procedures. Measure KR1 against all eligible issues, including those handled outside Meridian.

## 4. AI pressure-test notes

_Run the devil's-advocate prompt (in the Sprint 2 guide). Capture the verdict._

| Prompt question | What the AI surfaced | Change or defend? |
|---|---|---|
| Biggest assumption that could be wrong | Enterprise integration may benefit office teams more than field users. If foremen respond faster through texts, Meridian becomes another reporting obligation. | Change: observe job-site coordination and test foreman participation with minimal setup. Defend: field time savings, not record completeness alone, determine success. |
| The board question I can't yet answer | What evidence shows superintendents and foremen will voluntarily switch and save enough time to justify the investment? | Change: gather behavioral and time-use evidence in the pilot. The scenario establishes poor field adoption, not that issue coordination is the best entry point. |
| KRs that are outputs in disguise | None: the KRs measure behavior and reduced effort rather than delivery. However, mandated adoption could inflate KR1, and KR3 could improve office reporting while adding field effort. | Defend the outcome measures; use KR2 to check genuine field benefit and validate assumed baselines before committing. |
| The "no" I should reconsider | Excluding all analytics could remove essential visibility into unresolved issues by owner and age. | Change: clarify that basic field status visibility is included. Defend the exclusion of advanced enterprise analytics. |
| Strategy or wish list? Why? | A focused strategy with an unproven adoption mechanism: it names a user, workflow, advantage, and sacrifice but has not demonstrated why field teams will switch. | Defend the narrow focus and additive approach; test the adoption mechanism before scaling. |
